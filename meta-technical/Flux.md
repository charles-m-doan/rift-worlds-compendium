# Flux

Flux refers to the **local permeability state** of the substrate—how readily deep-layer resonance patterns propagate, stabilize, and interact with emergent-layer structures.  
It determines how strongly the Noesis-mediated substrate supports **resonance conduction**, **anchoring reinforcement**, and **projection viability**.

Flux is an environmental property, not a substance or force. It is very similar in behavior to gravitational curvature, but modulated across more and different dimensions.

---

# 1. Definition

Flux is:

> **The local responsiveness of the substrate to deep-layer resonance propagation, governing how easily structured computation can influence or cross the emergent-layer projection boundary.**

Flux modulates:

- resonance throughput,  
- anchoring efficiency (reduces anchoring but allows for more kinds of anchoring configurations),  
- projection scale and stability,  
- effective dimensionality of the emergent manifold.

Flux does not create mechanisms; it adjusts the **environmental conditions** under which they operate.

---

# 2. Dimensionality

Tetra is a stabilized, low-dimensional crystallization of deeper-layer adjacency patterns.  
Flux determines how rigid or permeable this crystallization is.

- **High Flux** → increased effective dimensionality (greater permeability to deeper modes)  
- **Low Flux** → reduced dimensionality (rigid projection boundary)

Dimensional fluctuation appears to Tetra-bound observers not as geometric warping but as:

- increased magical capacity,  
- altered physical behavior,  
- deeper-layer activity manifesting more readily.

Flux governs the **dimensional stiffness** of Tetra.

---

# 3. Effects on Anchoring

Anchoring depends on identity stability across recursive Noesis feedback.  
Flux modifies the strength of this feedback loop by introducing more degrees of freedom.
More degrees of freedom allows for more possible branches, which dilutes anchoring.
However, this also expands possible unique configurations. Higher flux means much higher anchoring potential, but lower baseline behavior.

- **Higher Flux** → weaker or unstable reinforcement  
- **Lower Flux** → higher anchoring reinforcement

Consequences:

- dissonance dissipates faster on average in high flux,
- anchors become slightly weaker in high flux, but also more amenable to enhancement,
- highly integrated entities (e.g., hypertronic organisms) elevate local Flux slightly.

Anchoring requirements do not change; their **effectiveness** does.

---

# 4. Mode-Dependent Flux

Flux need not be uniform across resonance modes.

Local substrate alignment can produce **mode-dependent Flux**, allowing:

- selective amplification of certain magical behaviors,  
- selective suppression of others,  
- resonance-targeted sealing or silencing,  
- devices tuned to a specific individual's resonance signature.

Flux is therefore functionally **multi-channel**, not purely scalar.

---

# 5. Local and Environmental Flux

Flux varies across spatial and structural scales.

### 5.1 Environmental Flux
Determined by large-scale substrate conditions, including:

- regional boundary stability,  
- accumulated anchoring density,  
- historical resonance patterns.

### 5.2 Local Flux Manipulation
Localized Flux elevation can be produced by:

- structured computation,  
- resonance scaffolds,  
- hypertronic artifacts or organisms.

Sustaining a local Flux deviation typically requires **continuous energy input** unless a stable configuration forms naturally.

---

# 6. Flux Waves

Flux changes can propagate as **waves**—time-varying fluctuations in dimensional permeability.

Flux waves may cause:

- temporary amplification or suppression of magic,  
- transient access to deeper-layer phenomena,  
- localized instability of physical behavior,  
- softening of the boundary between Tetra and Eidos.

Flux waves follow substrate-level propagation rules and do not alter fundamental mechanics.

---

# 7. Surges

A **surge** is a high-amplitude Flux wave, either localized or large-scale.

### 7.1 Caster-Induced Surges
With sufficient training, computation, and anchoring, an individual can generate a localized surge.  
This requires substantial ether expenditure to elevate local permeability.

A surge allows a fixed computational effort to produce disproportionately larger effects, up to environmental limits.

### 7.2 Exotic-Energy-Induced Surges
High-dimensional or exotic energy sources routinely produce large-scale surges, raising Flux across vast regions.

### 7.3 Flux Ceiling Constraints
Flux cannot be raised arbitrarily.

- Regions with **low baseline Flux** have higher dimensional rigidity.  
- Increasing Flux in such regions requires disproportionately more energy.  
- Large-area surges are limited by pre-existing environmental Flux.

This produces natural scaling and prevents unbounded amplification.

---

# 8. Dissonance and Flux

Dissonance results from failed stabilization of deep-layer resonance.  
Because dissonance represents unresolved resonance activity, it affects Flux directly.

### 8.1 Dissonance Raises Flux
Accumulated dissonance increases local resonance load, thereby raising Flux.

### 8.2 Mode-Selective Amplification
Dissonance elevates Flux **most strongly along resonance modes aligned with its own structure**.

Consequences:

- after intense magic use, environments become temporarily more permeable,  
- certain types of magic may become easier while others are unchanged or weakened,  
- dissonance-heavy zones can become unstable or prone to boundary softening.

Dissonance therefore provides a **feedback mechanism** between magical activity and environmental conditions.

---

# 9. Relation to Projection

Projection into Tetra requires:

1. Structured computation  
2. Resonance compatibility  
3. Anchoring stability  

Flux influences:

- the ease and scale of projection,  
- resistance to dissonance collapse,  
- the magnitude achievable from a given structured input.

Flux does not replace any requirement; it modulates **how favorable** the environment is for stable projection.

---

# 10. Summary

Flux is the **substrate permeability state** governing how readily deep-layer resonance interacts with emergent-layer structures.  
It determines:

- effective dimensionality,  
- anchoring potency,  
- projection efficiency,
- dissonance diffusion,  
- resonance selectivity,  
- and the behavior of Flux waves and surges.

Dissonance raises Flux, especially along aligned resonance modes, creating dynamic feedback between magical activity and environmental stability.

Flux modifies conditions;  
it does not introduce new mechanisms.
