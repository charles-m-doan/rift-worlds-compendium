## Magic as Identity Manipulation

Magic is defined as the manipulation of **identity**: its boundaries, continuity, structure, and relations.
All magical workings can be decomposed along multiple independent axes.

This document defines two of those axes:

- **Forms**, which describe *how identity is related to the self and space*
- **Aspects**, which describe *what kind of change is imposed on identity*

No working is purely one Form or one Aspect; all magic is a weighted composition of both.

---

## Forms  
*Topology of Identity Manipulation*

Forms describe **where identity is situated relative to the caster and how continuity is maintained**.
They are structural, not descriptive of effects, intent, or substance.

### Innervation
Identity reinforcement that remains within the caster’s locus field.

Innervation strengthens or excites existing identity structures without extending them.
It is characterized by maximal feedback, low risk, and strong embodiment.

Innervation is never absent from magic; it forms the baseline coherence that prevents collapse or untethering.

---

### Channeling
Continuous extension of identity beyond the core locus field.

Channeling maintains uninterrupted identity overlap while extending through space, structure, or medium.
Feedback remains bidirectional and responsive, allowing gradual correction.

Channeling trades safety for reach, and effort increases with distance, noise, and structural instability.

---

### Projection
Discontinuous extension of identity beyond continuous overlap.

Projection maintains identity coherence after locus-field continuity is lost.
Feedback may be delayed, indirect, or absent, requiring active stabilization.

Projection enables long-range and indirect effects but carries sharp failure modes and high dissonance risk.

---

## Aspects  
*Manner of Identity Alteration*

Aspects describe **what is being done to identity**, independent of where that identity is located.
They can be combined freely and expressed through any Form.

### Stabilization
Increasing identity coherence, persistence, or resistance to decay.

Stabilization underlies healing, enchantment longevity, ward durability, and dissonance suppression.

---

### Transmutation
Altering the internal structure or governing constraints of an identity.

Transmutation changes *how* an identity behaves, reorganizing its internal relationships without necessarily relocating it.

---

### Translation
Relocating identity patterns, states, or influence between substrates or loci.

Translation governs infusion, healing across bodies, enchantment transfer, and spatial displacement of effects.

---

### Binding
Coupling multiple identities into a shared constraint system.

Binding creates persistent relationships, sympathetic links, composite structures, and ward lattices.

---

### Disruption
Destabilizing, interrupting, or degrading identity coherence or continuity.

Disruption governs dispelling, interference, antimagic effects, and the collapse of hostile workings.

---

### Masking
Obscuring, diffusing, or misdirecting identity signatures.

Masking enables concealment, stealth, false landmarks, and anti-detection measures.

---

### Amplification
Increasing the magnitude or salience of identity expression without structural reconfiguration.

Amplification boosts strength, perception, signal clarity, and projection reliability.

---

## Notes

- All magic involves all Forms and all Aspects to varying degrees.
- Forms answer *where and how identity is extended*.
- Aspects answer *what kind of change identity undergoes*.
- Ontic composition and resonance behavior are handled separately under **Natures**.
