# Absolute Foundations

The foundations are the irreducible **preontological constraints** of the setting: the conditions that must hold for any ontology, law, or structure to arise or be describable. Everything else—physics, magic, matter, identity, spacetime, and history—must be derivable from or contingent upon what is stated here.

Nothing in the foundations is specific to any particular universe, era, species, or phenomenon. They do not describe *how* reality behaves, but what must already be true for *anything* to be describable at all. They are relevant to the setting because the setting itself can only exist by locating itself within the space of possibility defined by these constraints.

---

## 1. Distinction as the Only Primitive

The sole primitive of existence is **distinction**.

To exist is to be distinguishable.  
If no distinction can be drawn, no structure, relation, process, or entity can be said to exist.

Distinction does not require:
- space,
- time,
- matter,
- energy,
- causality,
- information,
- observers,
- or laws.

These all presuppose distinguishability and therefore cannot be primitive.

There is nothing “beneath” distinction.  
Any attempt to posit a deeper substrate already assumes the ability to distinguish that substrate from something else.

---

## 2. Distinction-Space (Platonia)

**Distinction-space** is the totality of all possible distinctions and relations among them.

It is not:
- a physical space,
- a container,
- a field,
- or a substance.

Distinction-space has:
- no geometry,
- no metric,
- no intrinsic dynamics,
- no time parameter,
- no causal direction.

It is pure structured possibility: the space of everything that *could* be distinguished, regardless of whether it ever stabilizes into a coherent ontology.

Distinction-space is sometimes called **Platonia**, but this name denotes scope, not structure.

---

## 3. Non-Spatial Adjacency

At the foundational level, adjacency is not spatial.

Two distinctions are adjacent if a change in one constrains, modifies, or conditions the other.

This form of adjacency:
- precedes distance,
- precedes locality,
- precedes geometry,
- and precedes causality as understood in physics.

Spatial adjacency is a later, emergent specialization of this more general notion.

---

## 4. Change Without Time

There is no fundamental time.

Change at the foundational level consists solely of:
- reconfiguration of distinctions,
- emergence or dissolution of relations,
- stabilization or destabilization of patterns.

Time is not a background parameter but an abstraction that arises when sufficiently stable update sequences can be coarse-grained into an ordered progression.

There is no universal clock, speed, or rate at this level.

---

## 5. Rejection of Fundamentality

No ontology is fundamental.

All ontologies are abstractions that persist only insofar as they remain coherent under ongoing reconfiguration.

There is:
- no privileged layer of reality,
- no final substrate,
- no ultimate building blocks,
- no timeless laws.

“Deeper” layers are not more real.  
They are only more explanatory in certain contexts.

Infinite descent is allowed and non-pathological.  
The absence of a final layer is not a flaw but a structural feature.

---

## 6. Substrate Independence of Structure

Structures are defined by their relational patterns, not by the substrate that realizes them.

The same structure may arise from:
- different underlying substrates,
- different realization mechanisms,
- or different ontological contexts.

No emergent layer has authority to define or constrain what lies beneath it.

An emergent ontology may be internally complete while remaining contingent and replaceable.

---

## 7. Stability as a Precondition for Meaning

Most distinctions do not persist.

In raw distinction-space, the overwhelming majority of possible configurations:
- fail to stabilize,
- dissolve immediately,
- or never form coherent relational patterns.

Meaningful structure requires **stability**:
- persistence across reconfiguration,
- resistance to combinatorial churn,
- and coherence under repeated update.

Stability is not guaranteed.
It must be earned.

This introduces the possibility—but not yet the mechanism—of:
- filtering,
- survivorship,
- emergence,
- and lawlike behavior.

---

## 8. What Is Not Assumed

The following concepts are explicitly *not* present at the level of Absolute Foundations:

- spacetime  
- matter or energy  
- fields or forces  
- probability  
- computation  
- information  
- observers or consciousness  
- identity  
- causality (in the physical sense)  
- laws of nature  
- magic  

These arise only after additional constraints, stabilizations, and abstractions are introduced.

---

## 9. Scope of These Foundations

These foundations do not describe a universe.  
They describe the conditions under which universes, laws, and phenomena can arise.

They do not predict outcomes.  
They constrain what kinds of explanations are admissible.

Everything downstream must respect these commitments or explicitly introduce additional assumptions beyond them.

---

## 10. Summary

- Distinction is the only primitive.  
- All ontology is abstraction.  
- No layer is final.  
- Structure is relational and substrate-independent.  
- Stability is rare and non-guaranteed.  

Everything else is contingent.
